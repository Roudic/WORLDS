# Project Riftwake

> Original working codename. This is not a cleared commercial title.

## Game Foundation v0.1

**Genre:** Single-player, party-based, cinematic tactical RPG  
**Engine target:** Unreal Engine 5 — PC project at `unreal/Riftwake/Riftwake.uproject` (C++ vertical slice; open on Windows with UE 5.4+)  
**View:** Third-person exploration with a tactical combat camera  
**Core fantasy:** Begin as a gifted but unproven fighter in a world where martial discipline, magic, technology, and unstable reality all collide. Build a party, make choices, master techniques, unlock original ascension forms, and rise from local conflicts to reality-shaping battles.

## The pitch

Project Riftwake is a reactive fantasy-action RPG with turn-based tactical battles. Characters can reach enormous power, but raw strength is only one way to change the world. Strategy, relationships, investigation, sacrifice, deception, and improbable dice outcomes can redirect entire story arcs.

The central rule is:

> Anything can happen, but everything does not have the same chance of happening.

A weaker hero cannot defeat a cosmic enemy with an ordinary lucky punch. They might survive, expose a weakness, activate an ancient device, unite the party, force a retreat, or create the opening that makes victory possible.

## Originality guardrails

The project can capture the broad appeal of explosive martial-arts fantasy and reactive tabletop role-playing without copying a protected universe.

### Keep

- Transformative power growth
- Martial arts mixed with ranged energy techniques
- Tactical party combat
- Dice-driven dialogue and world reactions
- Rivals, mentors, training, bonds, and breakthrough moments
- Large environmental stakes
- Choices that produce different allies, enemies, and endings

### Make completely original

- World history and cosmology
- Character names and designs
- Species, cultures, factions, and locations
- Energy terminology and visual language
- Transformations and their silhouettes
- Techniques, animations, sound, dialogue, music, and interface
- Story arcs, villains, artifacts, and mythical objects

### Visual rule

No character should be identifiable as a recolored version of an existing character. Ascensions should not depend on familiar hair-color changes, copied outfits, copied poses, or recreated attacks. Each lineage receives its own body language, geometry, materials, sound, and energy behavior.

Before commercial release, the final title, logos, character designs, and marketing language should receive a proper trademark and legal review.

# 1. The Hybrid World

## The world of Eidara

Eidara was created by **the Joining**, an event that fused several realities into one unstable world. Mountain kingdoms now border neon megacities. Living forests grow through abandoned starships. Ancient temples contain machines that remember futures that never happened. Some people were born after the Joining; others remember a world that technically no longer exists.

Reality is held together by an invisible field called **the Flux**. Living beings, spells, machines, and dimensional rifts all interact with the same field differently:

- **Pulse:** Flux generated and shaped inside a living body.
- **Aether:** Flux drawn from the world through symbols, rituals, and natural forces.
- **Lumen:** Flux captured, stored, and programmed by technology.
- **Riftforce:** Unstable Flux leaking between incompatible realities.

This gives the setting one understandable foundation while allowing martial artists, spellcasters, engineered soldiers, monsters, and machines to fight within the same rules.

## Central mystery

Official history says the Joining was a natural cosmic disaster. Evidence suggests that someone caused it. Across Eidara, ancient structures called **Axis Engines** are beginning to awaken. Each one can stabilize a region, merge it with another reality, or erase it completely.

The player gradually discovers that their unusual ability to combine different forms of Flux is connected to the Joining.

## Starting regions

### Crossfall

A crowded city built around the point where three realities collided. Stone keeps, suspended railways, martial academies, spell markets, and machine districts occupy the same skyline. Crossfall is the first hub and the location of the opening saga.

### The Cinderstep Marches

Volcanic highlands governed by rival combat houses. Its fighters practice Pulse disciplines based on breath, rhythm, balance, and controlled impact.

### The Verdant Deep

A living wilderness where memory alters geography. Aether practitioners negotiate with storms, rivers, beasts, and ancestral echoes rather than simply casting spells at them.

### The Glassline

A chain of brilliant machine cities powered by Lumen cores. Citizens can modify their bodies, build companions, and program combat effects, but the oldest machines have begun dreaming.

### Skygrave

Floating ruins and shattered pieces of worlds that never fully joined Eidara. Gravity, time, and identity behave unpredictably here.

### The Meridian Scar

A continent-sized Riftforce storm. Expeditions enter seeking lost people, alternate histories, impossible creatures, and dormant Axis Engines.

## Faction seeds

- **The Meridian Accord:** Wants to stabilize the joined world through diplomacy and controlled research.
- **The Pure Horizon:** Wants to separate the realities, even if millions of post-Joining people cease to exist.
- **The Crown Houses:** Martial dynasties competing to control Pulse knowledge and territory.
- **The Glass Synod:** Machine minds and engineers debating whether created life deserves sovereignty.
- **The Wild Choir:** Aether communities that believe Eidara is becoming a living consciousness.
- **The Unbound:** Rift-altered people who see instability as evolution rather than disaster.

# 2. Character Creation

Character identity has three layers instead of a rigid race-and-class combination.

## Origin

Origin determines biology, culture, starting traits, and how the world initially reacts to the character.

| Origin | Identity | Starting strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crossborn | People born among several merged cultures | Flexible skill selection and social adaptability |
| Ashbound | Highland communities adapted to heat and dense Flux | Durability and stored-impact techniques |
| Verdant Kin | People symbiotically linked to living Aether | Healing, senses, and environmental control |
| Ironwoken | Constructed people with independent souls | Modular body upgrades and status resistance |
| Lumenborn | Genetically and technologically engineered citizens | Precision, analysis, and energy efficiency |
| Riftmarked | Survivors permanently altered by unstable reality | Unpredictable powers and reality manipulation |

These are starting identities, not moral categories. Every origin contains conflicting cultures, beliefs, and personalities.

## Discipline

The player selects one primary discipline and can later blend in a second.

| Discipline | Combat identity |
| --- | --- |
| Vanguard | Close-range pressure, grapples, launches, and armor breaking |
| Channeler | Ranged Flux techniques, zones, blasts, and clashes |
| Slipstream | Speed, repositioning, aerial movement, and counters |
| Warden | Protection, interception, barriers, and enemy control |
| Weaver | Aether effects, summons, terrain changes, and conditions |
| Artificer | Lumen weapons, drones, traps, scans, and body upgrades |
| Envoy | Morale, bonds, commands, deception, and conflict resolution |

A Vanguard/Weaver might reinforce their body with living stone. An Artificer/Channeler might build programmable energy attacks. An Envoy/Warden might protect allies through commands and shared Resolve.

## Conviction

Conviction replaces a fixed alignment system. The player chooses two values, such as:

- Freedom
- Duty
- Mercy
- Strength
- Truth
- Belonging
- Ambition
- Balance

Choices can strengthen, challenge, or transform these convictions. Companions remember whether the player lived by their stated values.

# 3. Attributes and Resources

Core attributes normally range from 1 to 20. Ten represents a capable adult. Attributes can exceed 20 only through high-tier progression, equipment, or Ascension.

| Attribute | Controls |
| --- | --- |
| Might | Physical force, melee damage, lifting, and grappling |
| Agility | Movement, dodging, initiative, and aerial control |
| Control | Accuracy, Flux shaping, technique stability, and efficiency |
| Grit | Vitality, endurance, pain resistance, and overcharge safety |
| Intellect | Tactics, machines, investigation, medicine, and lore |
| Will | Concentration, fear resistance, Breakthroughs, and Ascension control |
| Presence | Persuasion, intimidation, leadership, deception, and empathy |

The familiar attribute modifier is:

```text
Modifier = floor((Attribute - 10) / 2)
```

## Derived resources

### Vitality

Physical ability to remain in the fight. Reaching zero does not automatically mean death. The final attack, story context, injuries, and player choices determine the outcome.

```text
Vitality = Origin Base + (Grit Modifier × 5) + Level Growth
```

### Flux

Universal power resource used for Pulse, Aether, Lumen, and Riftforce techniques.

```text
Flux = Discipline Base + (Control Modifier × 3) + (Will Modifier × 2)
```

### Guard

The target number for ordinary attacks.

```text
Guard = 10 + Agility Modifier + Gear + Active Effects
```

### Resolve

A scarce story resource. Resolve can reroll a die, resist a personality-altering effect, protect a companion, or activate a bond ability. Resolve returns through rest, major conviction choices, and meaningful companion scenes—not through ordinary consumables.

### Pressure

Pressure rises when a character takes heavy damage, sees an ally fall, risks a conviction, or deliberately overcharges. It can enable Breakthroughs, desperate techniques, and volatile Ascensions. High Pressure also makes failure more dangerous.

# 4. Dice and Consequences

## Core check

```text
d20 + Attribute Modifier + Proficiency + Situational Modifier
```

The roll is compared with a difficulty class or an opponent's defense.

| Difficulty | DC |
| --- | ---: |
| Routine under pressure | 8 |
| Trained challenge | 12 |
| Difficult | 16 |
| Exceptional | 20 |
| Legendary | 24 |
| Reality-defying | 28+ |

## Outcome ladder

| Result | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Miss by 10+ | Severe failure and major consequence |
| Miss by 1–9 | Failure, usually with a new route or cost |
| Meet or beat DC | Success |
| Beat DC by 5+ | Strong success with an added advantage |
| Beat DC by 10+ | Exceptional success that can change the scene |

A natural 20 upgrades a possible outcome by one step. A natural 1 downgrades it by one step. Neither overrides the logic of the world. A mortal cannot command a planet to move with one lucky Athletics roll.

## Advantage and disadvantage

Roll two d20s and keep the higher or lower result. Advantage should come from preparation, party assistance, environmental control, discovered secrets, techniques, and relationships—not only from character stats.

## The Rift Die

When reality, forbidden power, or an Axis Engine makes a scene volatile, roll a separate d6 after the normal check. The d20 decides whether the action succeeds. The Rift Die decides what unusual thing enters the fiction.

| d6 | Rift event |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Fracture: a new danger or reality breach appears |
| 2 | Cost: the result consumes extra Flux, time, equipment, or trust |
| 3 | Shift: the environment or objective changes |
| 4 | Opening: a new route, weakness, or conversation becomes available |
| 5 | Boon: an unexpected ally, memory, or resource intervenes |
| 6 | Anomaly: a rare event with long-term story consequences |

Rift events are authored possibilities, not unrestricted procedural randomness. Every scene receives a small table of consequences that make sense for its location and story.

## Fail-forward rule

Important failures should change the story instead of merely blocking it.

Examples:

- Failing to persuade a guard starts a chase that reveals a hidden entrance.
- Failing to stabilize a machine saves the district but awakens the mind inside it.
- Losing a tournament match changes the rival relationship and sends the player into an underground bracket.
- Failing to control an Ascension wins the immediate fight but damages trust with a companion.

# 5. Power and Scale

## Resonance Rating

Characters in Eidara use scanners, rituals, senses, and combat intuition to estimate **Resonance**. Resonance is the original-world equivalent of a visible power level. It communicates danger and growth without being used directly as damage.

```text
Displayed Resonance = Core Score × Scale Factor × Form Factor × Output × Condition
```

- **Core Score:** Training, level, potential, and technique mastery.
- **Scale Factor:** The character's current power band.
- **Form Factor:** Ascension, equipment, pact, or temporary state.
- **Output:** How much power the character is intentionally releasing.
- **Condition:** Fatigue, wounds, fear, terrain, and status effects.

Characters can suppress, disguise, misread, or temporarily spike their Resonance.

## Power bands

| Band | Scale factor | Fictional scope |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Mortal | ×1 | Skilled people and dangerous local creatures |
| Awakened | ×10 | Superhuman fighters and district-level threats |
| Ascendant | ×100 | Army-breaking champions and regional threats |
| World-Class | ×1,000 | Beings capable of reshaping nations or continents |
| Astral | ×10,000 | Planetary and dimensional forces |
| Sovereign | ×100,000 | Multi-world powers and Axis Engine masters |
| Mythic | ×1,000,000 | Reality-defining endgame entities |

The exact rating creates excitement and comparison. The band controls mechanical scale.

## Power-gap rules

- **Same band:** Normal combat rules.
- **One band lower:** Disadvantage on direct contests and reduced impact unless exploiting a weakness.
- **Two bands lower:** Ordinary attacks cannot cause meaningful damage. The weaker side needs a setup, artifact, team technique, environmental hazard, Ascension, or exposed vulnerability.
- **Three or more bands lower:** The immediate objective becomes survival, escape, negotiation, rescue, deception, or activating a world-scale countermeasure.

This preserves both enormous power differences and meaningful dice-driven possibilities.

# 6. Tactical Combat

## Turn structure

Each character receives:

- Movement
- One Main Action
- One Quick Action
- One Reaction per round

Main Actions include attacking, using a major technique, assisting, interacting with a major object, or preparing a powerful defense. Quick Actions include stance changes, minor items, scans, short movement techniques, and some companion commands.

## Tactical goals

Battles should rarely be empty damage races. Encounters can include:

- Protecting civilians
- Preventing an enemy transformation
- Escaping a collapsing area
- Breaking an energy anchor
- Convincing a rival during combat
- Separating fused enemies
- Holding out until an Axis Engine stabilizes
- Choosing whether to risk a populated environment with a powerful attack

## Technique structure

Every technique is a reusable data asset containing:

```text
Name
Discipline Tags
Action Cost
Flux Cost
Range and Area
Attack Attribute
Defense Target
Damage Dice
Impact / Stagger
Conditions
Environmental Interaction
Upgrade Nodes
Visual and Audio Identity
```

Technique categories include strikes, grapples, projectiles, beams, movement, barriers, counters, fields, summons, commands, scans, healing, and reality effects.

## Stagger and impact

Heavy attacks build Stagger separately from Vitality damage. Breaking the target's Stagger threshold can launch them, interrupt concentration, break armor, move them into hazards, or open a team combination.

This allows a defensive character to contribute by creating openings even when their raw damage is low.

## Clash sequence

When two compatible major techniques collide, combat briefly enters a three-beat contest.

Each beat, both sides choose one option:

- **Push:** Spend Flux and roll Control.
- **Overcharge:** Add power, gain Pressure, then roll Grit to avoid injury.
- **Redirect:** Use Technique or Intellect to change the clash angle.
- **Call for aid:** An ally spends their Reaction or Resolve to contribute.
- **Release:** Abandon the clash and attempt to evade the consequence.

Winning two beats resolves the clash. Margin of victory determines whether the loser is staggered, injured, launched, or exposed rather than automatically killed.

## Defeat states

At zero Vitality, a character can become unconscious, injured, captured, transformed, corrupted, separated, or dead depending on stakes and prior choices. Named-character death should be an authored possibility, not a random result from ordinary damage.

# 7. Ascension

Transformations are called **Ascensions**. They are personalized expressions of identity, training, biology, bonds, technology, or contact with the Rift.

## Ascension sources

- **Tempered:** Achieved through discipline and bodily mastery.
- **Symbiotic:** Shared with a creature, ecosystem, or living Aether.
- **Forged:** Produced by designed upgrades and programmable Lumen.
- **Covenant:** Granted through a negotiated bond with an external intelligence.
- **Catalytic:** Awakened by a defining choice, conviction, or relationship.
- **Riftborn:** Created through controlled exposure to alternate realities.

## Ascension data

Every Ascension defines:

```text
Unlock Conditions
Activation Requirement
Power-Band Shift
Attribute Changes
New Technique Tags
Flux Upkeep
Pressure Gain
Control Difficulty
Failure Consequence
Visual Language
Mastery Track
Story Reactions
```

## Mastery

Ascensions begin unstable. Mastery can:

- Reduce Flux upkeep
- Lower control difficulty
- Preserve dialogue options while transformed
- Prevent collateral damage
- Unlock alternate forms instead of simple stronger versions
- Allow partial transformation of a limb, sense, weapon, or ability
- Create party combination forms

## Breakthroughs

A Breakthrough is not a random free upgrade. The player must establish a **Catalyst** through earlier choices, training, bonds, discoveries, or sacrifices. During extreme Pressure, the player can roll Will to activate the prepared Catalyst.

Success may temporarily unlock an Ascension or complete its permanent awakening. Failure still releases power, but adds injury, instability, lost control, or a story consequence.

Breakthrough opportunities are limited by chapter and context, preventing players from farming near-death situations for unlimited power.

# 8. Progression

## Progression layers

| Layer | Earned through | Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Character Level | Quests, discovery, conflicts, and decisions | Attributes, traits, discipline nodes |
| Technique Mastery | Using a technique meaningfully and training it | Lower cost, new effects, alternate versions |
| Resonance Band | Major story milestones and controlled power trials | New scale of combat and exploration |
| Mentor Track | Training, trust, and completing a mentor's philosophy tests | Signature techniques and stances |
| Companion Bond | Shared choices, loyalty, conflict, and reconciliation | Team reactions, combination techniques, rescue actions |
| Ascension Mastery | Controlled transformation use and personal story progress | Efficiency, control, partial forms, new branches |
| World Knowledge | Investigation, lore, scanning, and faction work | Weaknesses, dialogue routes, crafting, alternate resolutions |

## Level philosophy

The first full campaign can use levels 1–20. Levels improve flexibility and consistency. Resonance bands represent the major leaps in destructive scale.

This prevents a level increase from automatically multiplying every number while still supporting dramatic power growth.

## No empty grinding

Repeating low-risk enemies gives little or no progression. The strongest growth comes from:

- Difficult training with a defined lesson
- New opponents and tactics
- Resolving personal conflicts
- Discovering how a power works
- Making costly conviction choices
- Mastering abilities under new conditions
- Combining disciplines creatively

# 9. Companions and Reactive Story

The active party contains the player plus three companions. Other recruited characters remain available at the hub, participate in story scenes, and can affect missions remotely.

Each companion tracks:

- Approval and trust
- Personal conviction
- Fear and ambition
- Relationship with every other companion
- Unresolved personal objective
- View of the player's power usage
- Willingness to follow dangerous commands

High approval does not mean blind agreement. A trusted companion may confront the player because they believe the relationship can survive honesty.

## Combat conversations

Dialogue can occur inside battles. Discovering the correct history, bringing a certain companion, sparing an earlier enemy, or demonstrating a conviction can unlock checks that alter the encounter.

Possible results include surrender, betrayal, temporary alliance, exposed weakness, transformed objective, interrupted Ascension, or a future rival relationship.

# 10. First Playable Saga

## Working title: The Crossfall Trials

**Target length:** 2–3 hours  
**Purpose:** Prove the complete game loop before building the larger world.

## Setup

Crossfall hosts a public competition designed to recruit protectors for regions destabilized by the Joining. The player enters for a personally selected reason: money, recognition, answers, duty, or access to restricted Axis research.

During the final trial, a Rift storm activates an Axis Engine buried below the arena. The machine begins overlaying alternate versions of Crossfall on top of the present city.

## Main cast placeholders

- **The Player:** A rare hybrid Flux user whose origin and convictions are customizable.
- **Sori Vale:** A Verdant field medic who hears the city's living memory. Values mercy but fears uncontrolled power.
- **Tamsin Rook:** An Ironwoken arena tactician seeking legal recognition as a person rather than property.
- **Ivo Renn:** A Lumen investigator whose missing sibling may exist in another version of the city.
- **Maelin Orr:** A respected mentor who teaches that control matters more than output.
- **Vexa Thorn:** A rival contestant who believes only overwhelming strength can protect a joined world.
- **The Custodian:** The awakened intelligence inside the Axis Engine. It is dangerous, frightened, and not necessarily evil.

All names and designs are placeholders subject to originality review.

## Flow

### Chapter 1: Arrival

- Character creation and chosen motivation
- Explore a compact Crossfall district
- Meet two possible companions
- Resolve entry through persuasion, investigation, payment, favor, or combat challenge
- Complete an optional mentor assessment that teaches dice and Resonance

### Chapter 2: The Trials

- Tactical introductory battle
- Environmental challenge with multiple skill solutions
- Rival scene affected by performance and dialogue
- Investigation into sabotaged Lumen equipment
- Second battle with an optional nonviolent resolution

### Chapter 3: Riftwake

- The arena and nearby district begin shifting between alternate versions
- The player chooses which civilians, companion, or research objective to prioritize
- Rift Die events begin
- Temporary Breakthrough opportunity becomes available if its Catalyst was established
- Final encounter against the Custodian, Vexa, or both depending on earlier choices

## Final resolutions

- **Destroy the Engine:** The immediate city is saved, but regional rifts worsen.
- **Stabilize the Engine:** Requires technical, magical, and companion preparation; the Custodian remains alive.
- **Synchronize with it:** The player gains a risky Rift Ascension and becomes politically dangerous.
- **Release it:** The Custodian leaves the city in a constructed body, creating a powerful future ally or enemy.
- **Lose the confrontation:** Crossfall partially changes, and the campaign continues in a more unstable version of the world.

There is no single perfect result. Preparation determines which costs can be avoided.

## Vertical-slice content budget

- One detailed hub district
- One arena with three combat configurations
- Three recruitable companions, with two available in combat during the slice
- One mentor
- One rival
- Six player techniques
- Three companion technique kits
- One starter Ascension
- Five dialogue-heavy scenes
- Three tactical encounters
- One environmental challenge
- One Clash sequence
- Four major ending states

# 11. Unreal Engine Foundation

## Recommended production approach

Use a hybrid implementation:

- **C++ or stable engine systems:** Attributes, dice math, save-state structure, gameplay tags, effect calculations, deterministic combat rules.
- **Blueprints and data assets:** Techniques, Ascensions, encounters, VFX hooks, dialogue events, quest reactions, and content tuning.

If the first developer is new to Unreal, the vertical slice can begin Blueprint-first using actor components. The rules should remain data-driven so the project can later adopt Unreal's Gameplay Ability System without rewriting story content.

## Core systems

### Character actor

Owns movement, animation, camera interaction, faction, party membership, and combat state.

### Attribute component

Stores base attributes, derived resources, current condition, power band, output percentage, and Resonance calculation.

### Dice subsystem

Receives a roll request and returns a complete result object:

```text
Roll Type
Raw Dice
Chosen Die
Attribute Modifier
Proficiency
Situation Modifiers
Target DC
Margin
Outcome Tier
Natural 1 / 20
Rift Die Result
Source Tags
```

All UI, dialogue, combat, and quest systems use the same subsystem.

### Technique data asset

Defines cost, targeting, tags, rolls, effects, upgrades, animation hooks, VFX hooks, AI value, and environmental behavior.

### Ascension data asset

Defines requirements, granted effects, form state, upkeep, control rolls, mastery, animation, and story tags.

### Combat director

Runs initiative, turns, reactions, encounter objectives, victory states, defeat states, combat conversations, and Clash transitions.

### Narrative state manager

Stores decisions as gameplay tags and values rather than hard-coded branches.

Examples:

```text
Choice.Crossfall.SavedEngineers
Choice.Trials.SparedSaboteur
Bond.Sori.Trust.High
Knowledge.Axis.CustodianName
Conviction.Mercy.Challenged
World.Crossfall.RiftLevel.2
```

### Save game

Persists character build, party state, quest tags, world state, relationships, discovered weaknesses, technique mastery, and Ascension control.

# 13. Immediate Design Decisions

The current foundation assumes:

- Single-player first
- Turn-based tactical combat
- Third-person exploration with a controllable tactical camera
- Custom protagonist with three active companions
- Dice visible for important checks, optional for routine combat rolls
- Original universe with no protected characters, locations, attacks, symbols, music, or visual recreations

The next production document should lock:

1. Whether the player directly controls every party member in combat.
2. Whether movement uses a grid, free measured movement, or fixed combat zones.
3. Whether the prototype is Blueprint-only or Blueprint plus C++.
4. The first six player techniques and their exact math.
5. The starter Ascension's visual identity, risk, and unlock Catalyst.

## Recommended next move

Build the **combat math prototype** before creating final characters or environments. It should allow two fighters to roll attacks, spend Flux, build Stagger, activate one Ascension, and resolve one Clash. Once that feels fun, the story and content can safely grow around it.
